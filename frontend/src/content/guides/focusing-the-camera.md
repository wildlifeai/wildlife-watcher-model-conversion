The Wildlife Watcher's main camera has a fixed **3.04 mm** lens (f/2.0) with a
~62° field of view. That combination is chosen for close-range monitoring of
small animals: positioned 30–50 cm above a platform, the camera captures the
whole platform at around 5–9 pixels per millimetre — enough detail to identify
small invertebrates and lizards.

| Camera height | Scene covered | Detail (full resolution) |
|---|---|---|
| 30 cm | ~36 × 27 cm | ~9 px/mm |
| 40 cm | ~48 × 36 cm | ~6.8 px/mm |
| 50 cm | ~60 × 45 cm | ~5.5 px/mm |

## Why focus matters at this distance

The lens ships focused for roughly **1 m to infinity**. A ground-facing camera
with a bait platform sits much closer than that, so images taken at the factory
focus will look soft. Adjusting the focus to your platform distance is a
do-it-once step during setup.

## Adjusting the focus

1. Mount the camera at its final height above the platform.
2. Open the mobile app, connect to the camera, and start the **live preview**.
3. Gently rotate the threaded lens barrel until the platform surface is sharp
   in the preview. Small rotations make a big difference at close range.
4. Place a textured object (a leaf, a coin) at platform level to judge
   sharpness more easily than against a plain surface.

> The focal length itself can't be swapped — there is no lens mount — but the
> focus adjustment above covers every supported mounting distance.

## Checking your work

After focusing, run a quick motion-detection test from the app and review the
captured frames. If subjects at the platform edge look softer than the centre,
that's normal at f/2.0; centre your bait or attractant where focus is best.
